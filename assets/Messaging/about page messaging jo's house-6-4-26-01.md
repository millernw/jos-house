## Opening

Jo's House is named after Jo Maley — Bryan's mother. She passed away in 2025, and the grief that followed was the kind that doesn't have easy answers. Bryan's father and aunt both passed later that same year. In the span of twelve months, Bryan lost three of the people closest to him.

He didn't know how to carry all of it. So he went to his bees.

---

## The Discovery

Bryan has kept bees for years on his family's homestead in northeast Indiana. During the hardest season of his life, he found himself returning to the hives again and again — not because he had to, but because something happened when he got close to them. The hum. The warmth. The smell of the hive. Something in him would quiet down. The anxiety would loosen its grip. The grief, still there, became something he could breathe through.

He started looking into why. What he found was that this wasn't just his experience. Bee bed therapy — the practice of lying near or above active beehives to absorb their sound, warmth, vibration, and aroma — has been practiced and studied around the world for decades. The science backed up what Bryan had felt firsthand: regular exposure to a hive has measurable effects on stress, anxiety, blood pressure, and overall wellbeing.

The nearest place to experience it was four hours away. And it was always booked.

That's when Bryan knew what he needed to build.

---

## Who Bryan Is

Bryan Maley has spent decades in law enforcement in northeast Indiana, serving in various roles that have put him at the center of some of the hardest moments in people's lives. He has also serves as a Sheriff's Department Chaplain, sitting with families in grief, walking alongside people in crisis, and providing care to those who had nowhere else to turn.

He is also a pastor and served as a missionary in Vermont. Faith has always been the foundation of how he leads, how he serves, and how he heals.

Bryan has seen, up close and over many years, what stress, trauma, grief, and loss do to people. He's lived it himself. And he's learned that healing rarely comes from a prescription pad or a one-size-fits-all solution. Sometimes it comes from something quieter. Something more unexpected.

For Bryan, it came from the bees.

---

## What Jo's House Is

Jo's House is part of the Maley family's working homestead. It's a place built on the belief that healing is possible, that nature has more to offer us than we often give it credit for, and that everyone — regardless of what they're carrying — deserves access to real rest.

The bee bed therapy rooms sit at the heart of the property, with active beehives beneath specially designed beds. Guests lie back for 45 minutes and let the hum, warmth, gentle vibration, and deep earthy aroma of the hive do what Bryan discovered it could do for him: create a calm that is hard to find anywhere else.

Beyond the bee beds, Jo's House is surrounded by a working farm that teaches homesteading and natural living. The Maleys also keep bees for honey production — raw, local honey that tends to sell out quickly, because once people taste it, they come back for more.

---

## Who This Is For

Jo's House was built with a specific kind of person in mind — someone who is worn down, stressed out, and running out of things to try. But Bryan also carries a deeper hope for this place.

He knows what it is to grieve. He knows what it is to lose someone suddenly and without warning, and to not know where to put that pain. Jo's House exists, in part, as a place for people walking through that kind of loss — especially those who have lost someone to suicide, or who are managing PTSD, high anxiety, or the kind of grief that doesn't show up on a doctor's chart but never really leaves.

This is a faith-rooted, family-run place. Everyone who walks through the door is treated like a neighbor, because in northeast Indiana, they usually are.

## A Note From Bryan

I didn't build Jo's House because I had a business plan. I built it because I needed it — and because I kept meeting people who needed it too.

My mother's name was Jo. Losing her, and then my father, and then my aunt, all in the same year, was more than I knew how to handle. But the bees helped me find my way through. Every time I felt the anxiety rising or the grief getting heavy, I'd go to the hives. And something would settle in me.

I've spent my career in law enforcement and ministry walking with people through their worst days. I know what it looks like when someone is out of options. I also know that healing is real — and that sometimes it shows up in forms you wouldn't expect.

If you're tired, or grieving, or just desperate for something that actually works, I'd love to have you out to the farm. Come meet the bees. Give yourself 45 minutes. And see what happens.

— Bryan Maley
Founder, Jo's House
