## Messaging Foundation

**Site-level framework:** StoryBrand (SB7)

**Why StoryBrand for Jo's House:**
This is a trust-heavy service business where the visitor has already tried everything else and arrived skeptical. StoryBrand positions Bryan as the guide and the visitor as the hero — which is exactly right, because bee bed therapy is unfamiliar and Bryan's personal story is the primary trust-builder. The entire pitch is a transformation narrative: exhausted and out of options → finally herself again. StoryBrand is built for exactly this dynamic.

**The core narrative:**
- The customer is: A worn-down person who works hard, carries a lot, and has quietly accepted that real rest might not be available to her
- Their problem is: "My mind won't shut off and I don't know how to make it stop" — every local option is clinical, generic, or more of the same
- The villain is: A culture of overstimulation that keeps the nervous system permanently on guard, and a wellness industry that keeps offering more things to do instead of space to simply be
- The cost of inaction is: Stress that doesn't stay the same — it grows. The mind gets louder. The body starts to carry it physically. And the longer nothing works, the easier it is to accept that this is just how life feels.
- We help by: Offering the only bee bed therapy experience in northeast Indiana — 45 minutes above an active hive, with nothing to do but rest — guided by a man who found his way through his own grief the same way
- Success looks like: Walking out with a body that's finally exhaled. Mental noise that quiets. Sleep that comes easier. Knowing that real rest is available to you.

**Strategic reframe — the real competitor is resignation:**
The visitor's competitive alternative is not another wellness center. It is doing nothing and accepting that this is just how life feels. The hero section should name that resignation as the real enemy.

**Section-level formulas:**

| Section | Formula | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Hero | PAS | Pain is the strongest hook — lead with it |
| Problem | PAS | Name the villain; validate her experience |
| Guide / Bryan | StoryBrand Guide | Empathy first, then authority |
| How It Works | StoryBrand 3-Step Plan | Sequential, simple, reduces anxiety about the unknown |
| Success | BAB | Paint the after state with specificity |
| Failure / Stakes | PAS reversed | What happens if nothing changes — use once, don't dwell |
| Transitional CTA | AIDA | Free course for visitors not ready to book |
| Final CTA | AIDA compressed | Single action, no distractions |

**Voice and tone:**
Warm, direct, and human — like a trusted neighbor talking to you at the kitchen table. Not a brand talking at a customer. Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon. No wellness-speak ("transform your journey," "unlock your potential"). No clinical language. Bryan's voice is pastoral, genuine, and slightly plain-spoken in the best way — lean into that.

**What this site must never sound like:**
Clinical, corporate, hype-driven, or spiritual in an exclusionary way. No "synergy," no "holistic wellness journey," no preachy faith language, no urgency-manufacturing, no testimonials that sound like they were written by the company.

## About Page Copy

**Page headline:** This Place Was Built Out of Loss, and Hope.

**Opening:**
Jo's House is named after Jo Maley — Bryan's mother. She passed away in 2025, and the grief that followed was the kind that doesn't have easy answers. Bryan's father and aunt both passed later that same year. In the span of twelve months, Bryan lost three of the people closest to him.

He didn't know how to carry all of it. So he went to his bees.

**The Discovery:**
Bryan has kept bees for years on his family's homestead in northeast Indiana. During the hardest season of his life, he found himself returning to the hives again and again — not because he had to, but because something happened when he got close to them. The hum. The warmth. The smell of the hive. Something in him would quiet down. The anxiety would loosen its grip. The grief, still there, became something he could breathe through.

He started looking into why. What he found was that this wasn't just his experience. Bee bed therapy — the practice of lying near or above active beehives to absorb their sound, warmth, vibration, and aroma — has been practiced and studied around the world for decades. The science backed up what Bryan had felt firsthand: regular exposure to a hive has measurable effects on stress, anxiety, blood pressure, and overall wellbeing.

The nearest place to experience it was four hours away. And it was always booked. That's when Bryan knew what he needed to build.

**Who Bryan Is:**
Bryan Maley has spent decades in law enforcement in northeast Indiana, serving in various roles that have put him at the center of some of the hardest moments in people's lives. He also serves as a Sheriff's Department Chaplain, sitting with families in grief, walking alongside people in crisis, and providing care to those who had nowhere else to turn.

He is also a pastor and served as a missionary in Vermont. Faith has always been the foundation of how he leads, how he serves, and how he heals.

Bryan has seen, up close and over many years, what stress, trauma, grief, and loss do to people. He's lived it himself. And he's learned that healing rarely comes from a prescription pad or a one-size-fits-all solution. Sometimes it comes from something quieter. Something more unexpected. For Bryan, it came from the bees.

**What Jo's House Is:**
Jo's House is part of the Maley family's working homestead. It's a place built on the belief that healing is possible, that nature has more to offer us than we often give it credit for, and that everyone — regardless of what they're carrying — deserves access to real rest.

The bee bed therapy rooms sit at the heart of the property, with active beehives beneath specially designed beds. Guests lie back for 45 minutes and let the hum, warmth, gentle vibration, and deep earthy aroma of the hive do what Bryan discovered it could do for him: create a calm that is hard to find anywhere else.

Beyond the bee beds, Jo's House is surrounded by a working farm that teaches homesteading and natural living. The Maleys also keep bees for honey production — raw, local honey that tends to sell out quickly, because once people taste it, they come back for more.

**Who This Is For:**
Jo's House was built with a specific kind of person in mind — someone who is worn down, stressed out, and running out of things to try. But Bryan also carries a deeper hope for this place.

He knows what it is to grieve. He knows what it is to lose someone suddenly and without warning, and to not know where to put that pain. Jo's House exists, in part, as a place for people walking through that kind of loss — especially those who have lost someone to suicide, or who are managing PTSD, high anxiety, or the kind of grief that doesn't show up on a doctor's chart but never really leaves.

This is a faith-rooted, family-run place. Everyone who walks through the door is treated like a neighbor, because in northeast Indiana, they usually are.

## Bee Bed Therapy Page Copy

**Page headline:** What Is Bee Bed Therapy?

**Subheadline:**
It's not what you're imagining. Here's the honest answer.

**What it is:**
Bee bed therapy — also called apitherapy or hive therapy — is the practice of lying on a specially designed bed positioned above an active beehive. You are fully clothed, completely safe, and never directly exposed to the bees. What you do experience is the hive itself: the deep, resonant hum of thousands of bees at work, gentle warmth rising from below, a subtle vibration that moves through the bed and into your body, and the rich, earthy-sweet aroma of propolis, beeswax, and honey.

For 45 minutes, you do nothing. You just rest.

**Why it works:**
The effects aren't accidental. Research into apitherapy and acoustic biology suggests that the hive's low-frequency vibration — typically around 200–400 Hz — resonates with the body in ways that activate the parasympathetic nervous system. In plain terms: it signals your body that it's safe to stop being on guard.

Studies and decades of anecdotal evidence from practitioners around the world point to measurable effects on stress hormone levels, blood pressure, anxiety, sleep quality, and overall sense of wellbeing. The aroma of the hive — propolis and beeswax — has its own documented antimicrobial and calming properties.

This isn't a fringe practice. It's been used in Eastern Europe for over a century. What's rare is having access to it right here in northeast Indiana.

**What a session at Jo's House looks like:**
When you arrive, Bryan will walk you out to the farm and introduce you to the property. He'll give you a short orientation, walk you through what to expect, and answer any questions before you get settled. No one lies on the bed feeling uncertain — that's not how Bryan runs things.

You'll lie on the bed for 45 minutes. Some people fall asleep. Some lie there thinking, and then realize at some point their thoughts have gone quiet. Most walk out saying they feel different than when they arrived — calmer, heavier in their limbs in a good way, clearer in their mind.

After your session, you're welcome to walk the farm, spend time with the hives, and take home a jar of raw local honey if any is available.

**Is it safe?**
Yes. You are never in contact with the bees. The hive is beneath the bed, sealed from the session space. Bryan is an experienced beekeeper and has managed hives for years. If you have a bee allergy, please disclose this when booking — we want everyone's visit to be safe and comfortable.
