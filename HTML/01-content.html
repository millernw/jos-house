Messaging Foundation

Site-level framework: StoryBrand (SB7)

Why StoryBrand for Jo's House: This is a trust-heavy service business where the visitor has already tried everything else and arrived skeptical. StoryBrand positions Bryan as the guide and the visitor as the hero — which is exactly right, because bee bed therapy is unfamiliar and Bryan's personal story is the primary trust-builder. The entire pitch is a transformation narrative: exhausted and out of options → finally herself again. StoryBrand is built for exactly this dynamic.

The core narrative:

  • The customer is: A worn-down person who works hard, carries a lot, and has quietly accepted that real rest might not be available to her
  • Their problem is: "My mind won't shut off and I don't know how to make it stop" — every local option is clinical, generic, or more of the same
  • The villain is: A culture of overstimulation that keeps the nervous system permanently on guard, and a wellness industry that keeps offering more things to do instead of space to simply be
  • The cost of inaction is: Stress that doesn't stay the same — it grows. The mind gets louder. The body starts to carry it physically. And the longer nothing works, the easier it is to accept that this is just how life feels.
  • We help by: Offering the only bee bed therapy experience in northeast Indiana — 45 minutes above an active hive, with nothing to do but rest — guided by a man who found his way through his own grief the same way
  • Success looks like: Walking out with a body that's finally exhaled. Mental noise that quiets. Sleep that comes easier. Knowing that real rest is available to you.

Strategic reframe — the real competitor is resignation: The visitor's competitive alternative is not another wellness center. It is doing nothing and accepting that this is just how life feels. The hero section should name that resignation as the real enemy.

Section-level formulas:

Voice and tone: Warm, direct, and human — like a trusted neighbor talking to you at the kitchen table. Not a brand talking at a customer. Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon. No wellness-speak ("transform your journey," "unlock your potential"). No clinical language. Bryan's voice is pastoral, genuine, and slightly plain-spoken in the best way — lean into that.

What this site must never sound like: Clinical, corporate, hype-driven, or spiritual in an exclusionary way. No "synergy," no "holistic wellness journey," no preachy faith language, no urgency-manufacturing, no testimonials that sound like they were written by the company.

He didn't know how to carry all of it. So he went to his bees.

The Discovery: Bryan has kept bees for years on his family's homestead in northeast Indiana. During the hardest season of his life, he found himself returning to the hives again and again — not because he had to, but because something happened when he got close to them. The hum. The warmth. The smell of the hive. Something in him would quiet down. The anxiety would loosen its grip. The grief, still there, became something he could breathe through.

He started looking into why. What he found was that this wasn't just his experience. Bee bed therapy — the practice of lying near or above active beehives to absorb their sound, warmth, vibration, and aroma — has been practiced and studied around the world for decades. The nearest place to experience it was four hours away. And it was always booked. That's when Bryan knew what he needed to build.

Bee Bed Therapy Page Copy

Page headline: What Is Bee Bed Therapy?

Subheadline: It's not what you're imagining. Here's the honest answer.

What it is: Bee bed therapy — also called apitherapy or hive therapy — is the practice of lying on a specially designed bed positioned above an active beehive. You are fully clothed, completely safe, and never directly exposed to the bees. What you do experience is the hive itself: the deep, resonant hum of thousands of bees at work, gentle warmth rising from below, a subtle vibration that moves through the bed and into your body, and the rich, earthy-sweet aroma of propolis, beeswax, and honey.

For 45 minutes, you do nothing. You just rest.

Why it works: The effects aren't accidental. Research into apitherapy and acoustic biology suggests that the hive's low-frequency vibration — typically around 200–400 Hz — resonates with the body in ways that activate the parasympathetic nervous system. In plain terms: it signals your body that it's safe to stop being on guard.

Studies and decades of anecdotal evidence from practitioners around the world point to measurable effects on stress hormone levels, blood pressure, anxiety, sleep quality, and overall sense of wellbeing. The aroma of the hive — propolis and beeswax — has its own documented antimicrobial and calming properties.

This isn't a fringe practice. It's been used in Eastern Europe for over a century. What's rare is having access to it right here in northeast Indiana.

What a session at Jo's House looks like: When you arrive, Bryan will walk you out to the farm and introduce you to the property. He'll give you a short orientation, walk you through what to expect, and answer any questions before you get settled. No one lies on the bed feeling uncertain — that's not how Bryan runs things.

You'll lie on the bed for 45 minutes. Some people fall asleep. Some lie there thinking, and then realize at some point their thoughts have gone quiet. Most walk out saying they feel different than when they arrived — calmer, heavier in their limbs in a good way, clearer in their mind.

After your session, you're welcome to walk the farm, spend time with the hives, and take home a jar of raw local honey if any is available.

Is it safe? Yes. You are never in contact with the bees. The hive is beneath the bed, sealed from the session space. Bryan is an experienced beekeeper and has managed hives for years. If you have a bee allergy, please disclose this when booking — we want everyone's visit to be safe and comfortable.
